SeaScout • iPhone + Apple Watch

Explore on iPhone. Navigate on your wrist.

Which version are you using?

The new iPhone Map tab, USGS/NOAA layers and Biscayne collection on this page apply to the SeaScout 1.0 build 16 TestFlight candidate. Build 14, the earlier App Review submission, has 1,575 reference records and four location packs; it does not contain these new additions. Check your installed build in TestFlight. These instructions do not indicate App Store approval or public availability of build 16.

Browse reefs and wrecks, save private positions, and review surface bearing and distance on iPhone and Apple Watch. Build 16 includes 1,665 published reference records across Florida: 1,015 within 100 statute miles of Hollywood, 220 mooring positions at 24 source locations around Key West and the Lower Keys, 340 freshwater records from the Florida Geological Survey, and 90 National Park Service mooring positions in Biscayne National Park. A separate selection contains 15 original NOAA charted points with source terms.

Explore the iPhone map — build 16

  1. Open the Map tab. Pan, pinch or use plus/minus to explore. Type icons distinguish reefs, wrecks, springs, caves and other points. Numbered clusters expand when tapped; closely spaced points can be opened in a list.
  2. Search by name, choose a type, or turn on Saved to show bookmarked sites and private points. Show all matches fits the matching records. Sites in view lists up to 100 matches nearest the map center; its distances are from that center.
  3. Tap Nearby for a one-time location lookup and allow location if requested. Search and type filters remain active. Browsing itself does not start continuous location tracking; you can pan manually if location is unavailable.
  4. Tap a pin or list result. Open Details, choose Start surface guidance on iPhone, or tap Select for Watch and wait for the Watch confirmation.

Choose a map layer — build 16

The layer button offers Standard and Satellite from Apple Maps, Topographic from USGS, and Seafloor relief from NOAA NCEI. USGS shows land contours in covered US areas. NOAA shows modeled land and seafloor shape with varying resolution and mixed vertical reference levels; its colors are not measured depth or clearance. NOAA relief is discovery and planning context, not a nautical chart or a navigation layer.

USGS and NOAA detail ends at zoom 16. Zoom out or change the layer if imagery is unavailable. Saved records remain available, but all map imagery depends on the providers and connectivity or incidental cache. Loading a Watch location pack does not download these images. Layer choices apply to the iPhone browse map. See map sources and limits and map-request privacy.

Choose a site and navigate on Watch

  1. On iPhone, search for a site such as Neptune Memorial Reef and open its details.
  2. Tap Select for Watch. Open SeaScout on the paired Watch and wait for Selected on Watch on the phone.
  3. On Watch, open Prepared target, choose the site, and tap Start surface guidance. Allow location access when asked.

The guidance screen shows distance, true target bearing, compass heading and a north-up map that fits your position and destination. T means true north; M means magnetic north. During GPS gaps, Last GPS identifies historical position and range with their age. Keep the Watch above water for a fresh position. The map's ellipsis opens session controls, including End navigation.

What do the buttons mean?

  • Save site bookmarks a site for later.
  • Select for Watch sends your chosen target and queues missing region data. A queued message is not a confirmed selection.
  • Prepare on Watch shows your selected destination and confirmation, a resend button and connection retry controls. Use Choose a location pack to add an area for your trip; the bundled catalogue alone does not populate your prepared areas.
  • Load to Watch queues the chosen area. Wait for Ready on Watch before treating the transfer as confirmed. Send again and Retry queued transfers retry delivery. On Watch, Offline sites shows explicitly loaded areas and the prepared target.
  • Start surface guidance starts location updates and guidance on the device where you tap it.

Load an area before your trip

Build 16 offers the five areas below. Build 14 has the same loading flow with the first four areas; Biscayne National Park is new in build 16.

  1. On iPhone, open Prepare and tap Choose a location pack.
  2. Choose Hollywood & South Florida (1,015 records), Key Largo & Upper Keys (250), Key West & Lower Keys (220), Florida springs & caves (340), or Biscayne National Park (90), then tap Load to Watch.
  3. Open SeaScout on your paired Watch. In Prepare, wait for the area's status to change from queued to Ready on Watch. A request is not a delivery receipt.
  4. On Watch, open Offline sites to see the loaded area and your prepared target. Requested areas remain listed after restarting; demo and internal source-pack rows stay hidden.

Area counts overlap: the 250 Upper Keys records are also included in Hollywood & South Florida. Lower Keys adds 220 individual mooring positions at 24 source locations, not 220 separate dive sites. Biscayne adds 90 individual moorings near historic wrecks, reefs and other locations; these are buoy positions, not wreck centers. Loading an area saves coordinates and source details; it does not download offline map imagery.

Find springs and freshwater features

Search for Ginnie Spring or Orange Grove, or use the Spring, Cave and Sinkhole filters. The Florida springs & caves pack contains 333 spring records, six cave-associated spring positions and Orange Grove sinkhole. Site details retain the agency's names, aliases, source comments and linked operator or state-park classification information.

Not every inventory record is accessible or diveable. A cave label identifies a documented spring system, not a surveyed entrance, underwater route or permission to enter. Current access and conditions remain unverified. SeaScout provides surface bearing and distance; it does not map cave passages or locate you underwater.

Know what the map shows

  • The 1,665 reference records comprise 765 FWC artificial-reef deployments, 470 NOAA mooring positions, 340 Florida Geological Survey freshwater records and 90 NPS Biscayne mooring positions. These are not 1,665 unique or independently verified dive sites. You can view surface bearing and distance to their published coordinates. This does not establish their accuracy, access or suitability for navigation; original source limitations remain applicable.
  • Surface guidance is also available for private coordinates, valid saved surface marks, and selected charted points after accepting their source terms. Charted points retain their original source information; independent geographic and field review remain pending. A saved boat mark is the boat’s position when recorded, not live boat tracking.
  • SeaScout is a surface navigation aid and reference tool. It is not an emergency service, dive computer, official nautical chart, or safe-route planner. A bearing does not establish safe access or current conditions.
  • Underwater GPS is unavailable. Location accuracy depends on the device, reception, and conditions. SeaScout does not calculate live depth, decompression, gas, or ascent guidance.
  • The reference catalogue and saved pins remain on the device. Apple Maps, USGS topographic and NOAA relief imagery depend on their providers and network access or available cache; offline map imagery is not guaranteed. The Watch uses its existing Apple basemap.
